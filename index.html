How is my personal inflation calculated?

The calculator estimates what each category's current spending would have cost a year ago, using that category's annual inflation rate. It then compares today's total spending with the estimated year-ago total.

For each category: Year-ago cost = current spending ÷ (1 + annual inflation rate ÷ 100).

Personal inflation (%) = [(total current spending ÷ total estimated year-ago cost) − 1] × 100.

Example: N$200 on food (4.0% annual inflation) and N$100 on clothing (0.4%) gives an estimated year-ago total of N$291.91 and personal inflation of approximately 2.77%. This method differs slightly from a simple spending-weighted average of category rates.

How is my estimated monthly price increase calculated?

We first estimate what the same purchases would have cost one year ago using each category's annual inflation rate.

Estimated monthly price increase (N$) = total current monthly spending − total estimated year-ago cost.

Example: If you currently spend N$200 on food and N$100 on clothing, the estimated year-ago costs are N$192.31 and N$99.60. The difference is approximately N$8.09 per month.

This is the estimated difference in the cost of the same purchases, not an additional bill, and not necessarily your actual increase in spending. The comparison uses annual price changes; it does not mean prices rose by this amount every month.

General advice for managing rising prices

Rising prices can put pressure on household budgets. Prioritise essential needs, distinguish necessary expenses from non-essential purchases, and reduce avoidable spending where possible. Compare prices, plan purchases, avoid unnecessary borrowing and review your budget regularly as prices and circumstances change. Aim to live within your means and save where possible.
